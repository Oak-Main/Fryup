# The Bonfire Night Fry-Up: Feeding a Crowd on the Fifth of November

Bonfire Night puts the fry-up to work twice: a standing breakfast before a cold morning of prep, and a hot bap-based tea served in the hour before the fireworks start on the fifth of November.

## Why the Fifth of November Belongs to the Fry-Up

Bonfire Night is a sausage occasion. Chipolatas go on the bonfire menu by tradition, and the same banger earns its place at breakfast: a batch of sausages, bacon and eggs cooked indoors, eaten warm, and finished before everyone heads out into the November dark. The oven-tray method does the heavy lifting — it is the same technique used for a full-english-for-a-crowd cook-up, applied to a Bonfire Night timetable.

## Plan the Day Around Two Sittings

1. **Bonfire breakfast (morning of 5 November):** a full plate for the household — sausages, back bacon, eggs, beans and toast — cooked in the usual order so everything lands hot.
2. **Fireworks tea (late afternoon):** bap-based service. Batch-cook chipolatas and bacon in oven trays, pile into buttered baps with brown sauce or ketchup, and hold in a 100°C oven until the first display.

## The Batch Oven-Tray Method for Bonfire Tea

1. **Preheat** the oven to 200°C (fan 180°C, gas mark 6) with two shallow trays inside.
2. **Chipolatas first:** spread across one tray in a single layer; cook 20-25 minutes, turning once, until evenly browned and steaming with no pink juices (core temperature **75°C**).
3. **Back bacon on the second tray** for the final 8-10 minutes so the fat renders without crisping too early.
4. **Baps and extras:** warm the baps cut-side down for the last 3 minutes; offer fried onions, brown sauce and mustard on the side.
5. **Serve in relays:** the first trays out go straight into baps; the rest hold covered in the low oven for up to 20 minutes.

## Food Safety for Outdoor Evenings

- Cook sausages and bacon fully indoors before service; outdoor grills and disposable barbecues run cooler than expected on a November night and extend the danger zone.
- Keep cooked baps held at 60°C or above, and serve within two hours.
- Cool any leftovers within 90 minutes, refrigerate at **4°C or lower**, and eat within two days — or repurpose next-morning into a bubble-and-squeak style hash.
- Keep the kitchen clear of coats, scarves and gloves drifting in and out with the crowd.

## The Fifth Made Simple

Cook once, serve twice: a proper breakfast for the household in the morning, a tray of sausage and bacon baps for the garden in the evening, and the washing-up done before the first rocket goes up.
